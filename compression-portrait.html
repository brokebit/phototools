Size of the nose tip and ears relative to the eyes, by camera distance

The lens does not reshape a face; the distance does. Up close, the nose is much nearer the camera than the ears, so it is drawn larger and the cheeks cover the ears. From farther back those distances even out and the face flattens. Focal length only decides how much of that view fills the frame.

Plain-text summary

Distances are measured from the camera's sensor plane to the eyes, with the camera at eye level. The head is a mannequin with average adult proportions; real faces vary, so read the numbers as a guide. "Ears showing" and "apparent head width" are measured from the rendered image.
